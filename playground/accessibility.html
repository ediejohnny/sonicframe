Accessibility

A sound is for everyone, and so is its wave. Accessibility is SonicFrame's first priority: the basics are always on, and the extras are one option away.

Always on

Every frame and player, with nothing to set:

  • The wave is a slider. It has role="slider", is reachable with Tab, and says its position in words ("0:12 of 0:45") through aria-valuetext.
  • The keys of the WAI-ARIA slider pattern. Arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End. See Keyboard.
  • Real buttons. Play, skip, speed, volume and zoom are <button> elements with names that change with their state ("Play", "Pause").
  • Focus is never lost. A zoom button at its limit is marked aria-disabled instead of disabled, so the focus stays on it.
  • Less motion is respected. No grow-in and no animated buttons for people who asked their system for less motion. See Motion.
  • Text you give stays text. Labels, captions and tooltips never become markup, and control or direction-changing characters are removed.

One option away

Everything that adds behavior lives in one group, a11y, arranged by place, and is off until you turn it on: 

createPlayer({
    container,
    src,
    a11y: {
        lang: 'pt-BR', // the language of every word the player says
        labels: { play: 'Ouvir' }, // your own names, over the language's
        description: { keys: true, button: true }, // the wave in words
        zoom: { announce: true }, // say the new view after a zoom
        minimap: { keys: true }, // the minimap as a keyboard slider
    },
});
Option What it gives Read more
a11y.lang English or Portuguese words (or 'auto', the page's language), and the player marked with lang so a screen reader speaks in that voice. Screen readers
a11y.labels Your own accessible names for every control. Your own words
a11y.description The sound's shape in words, read by the person's own screen reader. The wave in words
a11y.zoom.announce "Zoom 4×, 1:20 to 2:40" after a zoom by keys, buttons or fingers. Screen readers
a11y.minimap.keys The minimap as a slider of the part in view. The minimap

On the tag, the same options are attributes that start with a11y-: 

<sonic-player src="/note.ogg" a11y-lang="pt-BR" a11y-description="keys button"></sonic-player>

Your part

  • Name each player. label: 'Voice note from Ana, 0:42' tells a list of players apart.
  • Show the focus. Style :focus-visible on the wave's container; see the focus ring.
  • Mind the contrast. The built-in palettes pass WCAG contrast; check your own colors the same way.
  • Give words to what has words. A transcript or caption is still the best description of speech; the caption plugin shows one beside the wave.

How we check it

Every accessible behavior has a test: the roles, the values in words, the keys of each pattern, the names in each language and the live region. What a test cannot prove is how it feels with a real screen reader.

Use a screen reader? Your experience is the proof that counts. Tell us what works and what does not in a GitHub issue: which reader, which browser, and what you heard.
